01

The Challenge

Transform a legacy, feature-heavy government booking portal into a streamlined, user-friendly, and modern digital service — while maintaining trust and legal compliance.

02

Objective

The core objective of this redesign is to increase user adoption of the Online Ticket Reservation System (OTRS) by reducing friction, improving accessibility, and creating a modern visual language that aligns with contemporary e-commerce standards — like RedBus — while maintaining the official identity of TNSTC.

Analysis of the Old Screen

The existing site provided every function needed for bus ticket booking. But the experience felt visually outdated and information-heavy — key actions competed with secondary content, and inconsistent layouts raised the cognitive effort needed to complete a booking.

Cognitive Load

The old hero section is overwhelming. The user is presented with a booking widget, massive destination cards, a scrolling ticker, and multiple contact numbers — all competing for attention.

Navigational Complexity

The top menu — Operator Login, Services, Login/Sign Up — is fragmented and difficult to prioritize based on a primary booking journey.

Cluttered Information

The search results page is dense with text, making it hard to scan for specific prices, departure times, or available seats. Typography is too small and lacks contrast.

Trust & Branding

While the official seal is present, the overall aesthetic feels dated and less secure compared to commercial competitors.

The Redesign Approach

The goal of the redesign was not simply to modernize the interface, but to create a clearer and more intuitive booking experience — improving visual hierarchy, simplifying the information architecture, and guiding users through each stage of the booking journey.
  • Simplifying the layout to reduce cognitive load.
  • Separating key tasks — seat selection, journey details, passenger information, and payment.
  • Improving visual hierarchy so users know what to do next.
  • Adding clearer information, such as selected seats, fare, and booking details.
  • Reducing uncertainty by addressing common user questions and potential errors.
  • Creating a more structured, intuitive flow from select to review to enter details to pay.

UX Improvements — Summary

Reduced Cognitive Load

The interface was simplified by prioritizing essential information and reducing unnecessary visual competition.

Improved Information Architecture

Content was reorganized based on user priorities — starting with ticket search and booking before moving into supporting information.

Guided Booking Journey

The booking experience was redesigned into a clear progression: Select Journey → Choose Seat → Add Passenger Details → Make Payment.

Better Error Prevention

Form fields and booking information were grouped and structured more clearly to reduce input mistakes.

Improved Scannability

Typography, spacing, cards, and content grouping make it easier for users to quickly scan information.

Clearer Action Hierarchy

Primary CTAs were visually prioritized, helping users understand the next step at every stage.
