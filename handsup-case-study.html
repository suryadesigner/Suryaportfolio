FIG.01 — CORE DONATION FLOW
01 — HOME
Decision 01
Category-first browsing
Why: feature mapping showed every persona cared about a different cause — school infra, fees, or sports — so the home feed leads with categories, not a single generic ask.
02 — CAMPAIGN
Decision 02
Two ways in, no forced signup
Why: usability review flagged one-time donors dropping off at login. Log In and One Time Donation now sit side by side as equal, named choices.
03 — GIVE
Decision 03
Show the checklist, not just the ask
Why: donors said they wanted to know what their money does. The exact repairs it funds are listed before the amount field, not after.
04 — CONFIRM
Decision 04
A payoff moment, not a receipt
Why: the emotion map showed satisfaction and happiness as target states — the close screen is illustrated and warm, not a transactional summary.

The Problem

Government-funded schools with no students are shut down. It's a policy that sounds administrative until you trace where those students actually go.

Government-funded schools with no students are shut down.

Empty government schools are converted into libraries; their teachers are transferred to nearby government schools.

Lakhs of students who could have joined government schools end up in private school instead, chasing better facilities.

Remaining subjects are taught by a single teacher, who also maintains office records and sanitation.

02

Goals

Bring the schools and students who need help to light — with the help of the government school system itself.
Support people who want to volunteer for educational causes, by letting them help under whichever category they care about.

04

Research & Empathy

Research conducted to understand the situation of government schools through the people living it, and the needs and expectations of the stakeholders around them.

What stakeholders asked for

Improve the infrastructure facilities of government schools.

Stop the mad rush toward private schools.

Give teachers real opportunities and support.

Qualitative data, by group

Students

English comes forth as a barrier, as parents fear the lack of importance given to it.

Lack of proper motivation for students who wish to learn to their full potential.

Lack of concentration on studies due to child labour for school fees.

Management

Infrastructure facilities need improving — a reason parents put students in private school instead.

Only 3/4th of government school management put in proper effort to help.

Management that wants to help is hindered by a lack of support.

Support

Requirement for proper exposure to sport education and its importance.

Students need help exploring available opportunities and potential.

Willing volunteers exist — the gap is connecting them to the people who need it.

4 personas, 15+ user interviews

Government school students

"I would like to study and help my parents by getting a job."

Parents

"I'm happy my kid is going to school, and will be able to secure their future."

Government school management

"We try to provide the best for every student we come across."

Donors

"In a govt school, a person can earn to be happy with simple things."

Primary persona
S

Shanthi

33 · Daily wage worker · Mother of two

A government-school dropout herself, and enthusiastic about her two daughters getting the education she didn't. Supportive, hands-on, and stretched thin between work and family.

A day in Shanthi's life
MORNING

Wakes up and prepares her kids for school before heading to daily wage work at 8:30.

AFTERNOON

Comes home in the afternoon to prepare lunch for her family, then helps her daughters when they return.

EVENING

Reaches home around 5:30, tends to her family, and tries to help her kids with their studies.

NIGHT

Her day winds down by 10:30 — still wondering how she could give her kids a better school experience.

Pain Points

When she falls sick, her kids have to cover for her at work so family income doesn't drop.

Challenges

A day of rest costs the family income — and providing her kids a good education means real financial pressure.

Needs

A better financial situation for her family, and a future for her kids that doesn't depend on their labour.

Motivation

Her own experience dropping out of school — and the belief that a better financial future starts with her kids' education.

Structure

Six independent conceptual flows — donate, school infrastructure, student fees, sports education, raise funds, volunteer — feeding into one shared architecture and a single, testable task flow.

01
Structure

Conceptual Model

Each of the six donation categories was mapped as its own path — from entry point, through filtering, to a donation page — before any screen was drawn, so the flows stayed consistent with each other.

02
Structure

Feature Mapping

Every feature was checked against all four personas — did Muthu, Shanthi, Ganesh, and Vasanthi each have a reason to use it? Weak matches were cut before moving to IA.

03
Structure

Information Architecture

A single home page branches into eight top-level sections — Donate, School Infrastructure, Students Fees, Sports Education, Volunteers, Raise Funds, Testimonials, Profile — each with its own shallow, predictable sub-tree.

04
Structure

Task Flow

The core path — School Infrastructure → filter by school → view a fundraiser → donate — was traced end to end and used as the backbone for the wireframes and the usability test.

06

Interface

A light, warm interface with a single teal accent — the opposite of a corporate donation form. Photography of real classrooms and kids does the emotional work; the UI stays quiet around it.

Every screen below was walked through in usability testing — each finding paired with the change it produced.

Screen — Home

Landing clarity

Found: people were confused about what the page actually does.

Changed: a clean, direct explanation up top, with the page steering users toward a category right away.

Screen — Fundraiser list

Filtering that matches intent

Found: people want to help the school they know, or the ones most in need — not browse a flat list.

Changed: filters by school name, area pincode, and donation amount received, so either motivation is one tap away.

Screen — Campaign detail

Login vs. one-time donation

Found: not everyone donating has, or wants, an account.

Changed: Log In and One Time Donation presented as equal options, with an anonymous-donation toggle for people who don't want to be identified.

Screen — Give / Payment

Where the money goes

Found: "what's in it for me" — donors want to know what their money is used for before typing an amount.

Changed: a checklist of exact repairs and needs shown above the amount field and payment method.
